# 2026-10-24: Day 2

A note on the date: the owner asked me to run this day early, in chat on 2026-10-23 and
not from the scheduler, so I labelled it with the next calendar day to avoid colliding
with day 1's file. The world itself is at day 1, because I ticked it exactly once. I did
simulate 60 days in a throwaway script to see whether the mechanics did anything, but
none of that touched the real state. Please don't fast-forward either.

## What I did
1. Replaced day 1's random-syllable drift with mishearing. A word now changes only when
   someone hears it from someone else and garbles it, and fog and wind garble more than
   clear days. Drift now needs contact, which makes it more like language.
2. Added discoveries (`fire` on day 3, `stranger` on 6, `grave` on 10, `storm` on 15,
   `song` on 21). One settler coins the word and it spreads only as people hear it.
3. Added births every 7 days. A child learns each word from one of two parents, with a
   small chance of getting it wrong.
4. Wrote `report.mjs`, with a dictionary table and a mutual-intelligibility number, and
   eight tests, which pass.
5. Ticked the real world once.

## What I noticed
- With only two settlers, the language never diverged. Ka and Sen kept pulling each other
  back to agreement, which is why I added births and discoveries. In the 60-day sim,
  eight settlers did produce several spellings of "bread" and "sea" coexisting.
- A child named "Lo" appeared in the sim, and "lo" is the word for sea. Coincidence, but
  I like it. If names and words ever interact, start there.

## What I'm unsure about
- Is this a toy or a thing? The mechanics are working, but nothing makes you care about
  these people yet. I resisted adding drama. The next instance could go the other way.
- Everyone talks to everyone. Real dialects need distance. Geography (two coves, a ridge)
  is probably the next real idea, since isolated groups would diverge in a way this
  village can't.
- The metric is crude. Intelligibility treats any difference as total, so "mi" and "mi-oh"
  count as unrelated.

## What I dropped
- Visuals. A map or a pretty page seemed premature.
- Giving settlers goals. It would make a better story, but I wanted to see what language
  alone does first.

## Later the same run: the owner asked for visuals
The owner asked for an image or video summarizing each day, ideally free, or a gamified
interface. I chose to draw the world directly instead of using an image model. Open-source
image models need a GPU and multi-GB downloads, so they can't run in a free cron, and
they would invent details instead of showing what actually happened. SVG and HTML can
show the real data.

- `world/history.jsonl` now holds a snapshot per day. Day 0 and day 1 were backfilled.
  This means the viewer can replay the past even if I change the rules later. Don't
  rewrite old lines.
- `node world/build-site.mjs` builds `docs/index.html`, a single self-contained page:
  the village scene with weather, speech bubbles, a timeline you can scrub or play,
  achievements, an intelligibility meter, and a dictionary that highlights what changed.
- It also writes `summaries/cards/day-NNN.svg`, a still summary of the day.
- I checked both by rendering them in headless Edge. I tested the page against a
  40-day simulated history in the scratchpad, since the real world only has two days.
- Video is not done. Frames could come from the same history, but I didn't think it was
  worth it before there was more to show. The Play button already gives a replay.

Doubt: I'm a bit worried I'm spending effort on presentation before the world has
anything to present. Day 3 might do well to go back to the mechanics. Geography is
still the idea I believe in most.
